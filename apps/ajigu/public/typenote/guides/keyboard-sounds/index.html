How to add sound to your Mac keyboard

Typing is a rhythm you already make all day. Giving it a pitch turns mechanical work into something oddly satisfying—and it takes about two minutes to set up.

Why keyboard sounds work

The click of a mechanical keyboard is satisfying because it confirms each press. A pitched note goes further: your brain starts to hear progress. Writers use it for focus, programmers for flow, and some people just like pretending their desk is a piano.

The easy way: TypeNote

  1. Download TypeNote (free, open source) and drag it to Applications.
  2. Open it—it lives in your macOS menu bar.
  3. Pick an instrument from 128 General MIDI sounds: piano, plucks, ocean waves, and more.
  4. Start typing. Every keystroke plays a note, with under 8 milliseconds of latency, in any app.

Beyond single notes

TypeNote can also load melodies. Write a tune in numbered notation (1–7), MusicXML, or MIDI, and your keyboard plays it back in order as you type. A smart mute keeps it quiet when you are on a call or playing media.

Quick answers

Does it slow down typing? No. Sound is generated locally with under 8ms latency—you will not notice it.

Is it free? Yes. TypeNote is free and open source, for macOS 14 and later.
